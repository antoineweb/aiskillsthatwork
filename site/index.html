Product demo guide

Turns a flow in your product into an animated step-by-step guide, then exports it as a video. The interface stays recognizable but is simplified and enlarged, your branding frames it, and the steps appear on the right as the cursor does the work.

Made and exported with this skill, for Marlo, a fictional invoicing app. 68 seconds, six steps, demo data only.

You give it

  • The flow, as screenshots or a description
  • The exact labels on your buttons and fields
  • Your logo and colours

You get

  • A storyboard to approve before anything is built
  • A page that plays in any browser, with pause and scrub
  • An MP4, every frame computed, not screen recorded

Video formats

  • Very high, 3840 x 2160 at 60 fps
  • High, 2560 x 1440 at 60 fps, the default
  • Medium, 1920 x 1080 at 60 fps
  • Low, 1280 x 720 at 30 fps

Voice to video

Turns a raw voice recording into a finished, captioned video scheduled to your channel. Silence-cut with pacing presets, local transcription, discreet captions, a frame-by-frame QA pass, and a review gate before anything goes live.

You give it

  • A voice recording, any length
  • Background footage or still images
  • A scheduling endpoint for your platform

You get

  • A wide or vertical video, voice clear over ambience
  • Discreet captions cut from the real speech
  • A scheduled post, only after your explicit go

The gates that matter

  • Privacy first: third parties are cut, never aired
  • Noise detection: hallucinated transcripts never ship
  • Nothing publishes without the owner's approval

Built on a real publishing pipeline: more than 60 rendered videos and daily automated slots. Works with any offline whisper and any scheduler with an upload endpoint.

How a run goes

  1. Describe the flow

    Say which task to teach and share the screens. The skill asks only for what is missing and never invents a label.

  2. Approve the storyboard

    You see every scene, its duration and the lines on screen. Change anything before a single frame exists.

  3. Get the page and the video

    One self-contained page to share, and an MP4 in the format you pick, checked frame by frame.
